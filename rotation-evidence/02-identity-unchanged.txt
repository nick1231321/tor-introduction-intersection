EVIDENCE 2 - THE IDENTITY DOES NOT CHANGE
=========================================

2a. THE AUTH KEY IS UNCHANGED ACROSS EVERY ROTATION
---------------------------------------------------
The auth key is what a relay uses to key an introduction point and what the descriptor
publishes. If rotation changed it, the descriptor would have to change too.
Each rotation prints the auth key three times: at START, at ESTABLISHED, and at SWAPPED.

successor; the rest are earlier successors that tor retired on its own schedule (see 2b).
The counts are per-key line totals; a key that rotated N times appears in about 3N lines.
What matters is that no rotation ever changed a key: every START/ESTABLISHED/SWAPPED trio
for one rotation carries the same key, and a key's intro point is fixed for the key's life.

tor's OWN time-period descriptor rotations (upstream, unrelated to this patch):
  Sep 20 19:56:17.318  rotate_all_descriptors
  Sep 20 20:04:18.235  rotate_all_descriptors

Each of those rebuilds the 'next' descriptor, which picks 3 brand-new introduction
points with brand-new auth keys. That is why the total number of auth keys seen grows
by 3 at each one. It is upstream tor behaviour, not an effect of the rotation patch:
no auth key ever CHANGED during a rotation - new keys only ever appear alongside a
rotate_all_descriptors event, and every key above keeps one fixed intro point for its
whole life, however many times its circuit was rebuilt.

2c. NO INTRODUCTION POINT WAS EVER RETIRED (obstacle 2 - the retry cap)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
MAX_INTRO_POINT_CIRCUIT_RETRIES is 3. If deliberate rotations counted as retries, an
intro point would be retired after 3 rotations and the descriptor would change.
Rotation depth actually reached, per rotation number:
  12 rotation #1
   9 rotation #2
  10 rotation #3
   9 rotation #4
   5 rotation #5
   3 rotation #6
   3 rotation #7
   3 rotation #8

Highest rotation number reached: 8  (cap is 3)
Lines mentioning removing/retiring an intro point:
  grep -icE "remov(e|ing) .*intro|retir|MAX_INTRO" notice.log  -> 0

2d. THE PUBLISHED DESCRIPTOR IS BYTE-IDENTICAL ACROSS A ROTATION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Captured around the 20:02:13 rotation cycle (before at 20:02:05, after at 20:02:25).

  service-side revision-counter before : revision-counter 525999
  service-side revision-counter after  : revision-counter 525999
  client-fetched revision-counter before: revision-counter 664457
  client-fetched revision-counter after : revision-counter 664457

The descriptor the CLIENT actually fetched and used is byte-identical before and after:
  sha256 before: 2b2ee87161ff9211bd86f273fe3f7c98e52c5246507c140240765c0ee7b6ce53
  sha256 after : 2b2ee87161ff9211bd86f273fe3f7c98e52c5246507c140240765c0ee7b6ce53
  diff         : no differences

CAVEAT, measured not assumed: the SERVICE-side dump (GETINFO hs/service/desc) does differ
in its superencrypted blob and signature across the two captures. That is NOT a descriptor
change - the service re-encodes and re-signs the descriptor on every GETINFO, with fresh
randomness. Control test in 02-desc-CONTROL-no-rotation.txt: two dumps taken 3 seconds
apart with NO rotation in between also differ in ciphertext and signature, while
revision-counter and the signing-key certificate stay identical. The revision counter is
the field that would increment on a real descriptor change, and it did not move.
