WHY IT FAILED - traced and verified in this tree, not guessed
------------------------------------------------------------
The replacement circuit exceeded tor's adaptive circuit-build timeout while it was still
building, and tor then kept it alive purely to measure how long it would have taken:

  src/core/or/circuitstats.c:636  circuit_build_times_mark_circ_as_measurement_only()
      "Mark this circuit as timed out, but change its purpose so that it continues to
       build, allowing us to measure its full build time."
      -> emits CIRC_EVENT_FAILED with END_CIRC_REASON_TIMEOUT
      -> circuit_change_purpose(circ, CIRCUIT_PURPOSE_C_MEASURE_TIMEOUT)

  src/core/or/circuitbuild.c:1111-1112  circuit_build_no_more_hops()
      /* We're done with measurement circuits here. Just close them */
      if (circ->base_.purpose == CIRCUIT_PURPOSE_C_MEASURE_TIMEOUT)
        circuit_mark_for_close(TO_CIRCUIT(circ), END_CIRC_REASON_FINISHED);   /* = 9 */

That is exactly the pair of records observed: the control port logged the circuit as
FAILED with REASON=TIMEOUT while still in HS_STATE=HSSI_CONNECTING with only 3 of its 4
hops done, and info.log then shows it closed at circuitbuild.c:1112 with reason 9. Four
such timeouts occurred in the 360 s cost window (see 04-cost.txt); the raw control-port
events are in 04-cost-rotation-ON.txt.

So this is ordinary circuit-build timeout on a loaded local network, not a defect in the
rotation logic - and because of make-before-break it costs nothing but a skipped cycle.
