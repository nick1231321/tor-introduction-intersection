EVIDENCE 6 - THE ROTATION PERIOD NOW DEPENDS ONLY ON THE CONFIGURED PARAMETER
=============================================================================

The problem, restated
---------------------
Evidence 3 and note 3 in README.md recorded that some rotation attempts did not
complete: the replacement introduction circuit hit tor's ADAPTIVE circuit-build
timeout while it was still building, and tor converted it into a build-time
measurement circuit. Nothing broke - the old circuit was still serving, which is what
make-before-break is for - but that rebuild was lost, and the intro point kept its old
path for another whole period. The effective rotation interval therefore became a
MULTIPLE of HiddenServiceIntroCircuitRotation, at a rate decided by the network, not by
the operator.

This file measures that before and after the fix, from the service's own logs.

Both runs: the same private Chutney network (`networks/hs-v3-rotation`), the same onion
service `xk335k6vqeexv3qpnn3jlvlbee4vcgp3lccmiusq5qem3wpxmr6cu7yd.onion`, the same
`HiddenServiceIntroCircuitRotation 120`, 6 introduction points live at a time
(3 for the descriptor clients are using, 3 for its successor).

  BEFORE : tor built at commit b63417722 (branch tip before the fix)
           2026-09-20 20:21:12 .. 2026-09-21 13:30:50   (17 h 09 m)
           logs/notice.log.period-BEFORE.gz
  AFTER  : tor built at commit f68858718 (the fix)
           2026-09-21 13:40:50 .. 2026-09-21 14:54:18   (73.5 min; 195 completed
           rebuilds over 6 live introduction points, i.e. about 33 full rotation
           cycles, and 36 elapsed 120-second periods)
           logs/notice.log.period-AFTER

6b. WHY THEY WERE LOST, AND WHY THEY ARE NOT ANY MORE
------------------------------------------------------
The adaptive timeout can convert a building circuit into a measurement-only circuit in
two places. Each logs a distinguishable line at info level:

  circuit_build_times_handle_completed_hop()  circuitstats.c  "Deciding to timeout circuit N"
  circuit_expire_building()                   circuituse.c    "Deciding to count the timeout for circuit N"

Those lines print tor's GLOBAL circuit id; the `[intro-rotation]` notices print the WIRE
circuit id. The two are joined through the `origin_circuit_new: Circuit N chose an idle
timeout` line that immediately precedes each `BUILDING` notice (hs_circ_launch_intro_point()
logs BUILDING right after the circuit is created). Script:
`rotation-timeout-correlate.py`.

  BEFORE  (logs/info.log.period-BEFORE.timeout-lines.txt.gz)
    replacement introduction circuits launched          2899
      established                                       2488
      never established                                  411
    "Deciding to timeout circuit N"                      860 distinct circuits
    "Deciding to count the timeout for circuit N"          0 distinct circuits
    LOST rebuilds whose circuit the timeout claimed   404 / 411   (98.3%)
    COMPLETED rebuilds whose circuit it claimed         0 / 2488

  AFTER   (logs/info.log.period-AFTER.relevant-lines.txt.gz)
    replacement introduction circuits launched           201
      established                                        195
      never established                                    6
    "Deciding to timeout circuit N"                       62 distinct circuits
    "Deciding to count the timeout for circuit N"          0 distinct circuits
    LOST rebuilds whose circuit the timeout claimed     0 / 6
    COMPLETED rebuilds whose circuit it claimed         0 / 195

  -> Before: 404 of the 411 lost rebuilds were killed by the adaptive timeout, and not
     one of the 2488 successful ones was. That is the cause, isolated.
  -> After : 0 of 201 replacement circuits were touched by it.
  -> The timeout is NOT disabled: 62 other circuits were still converted during the
     AFTER run, and the learned value is still being updated (423 updates,
     "Set circuit build timeout to 16..17ms"). The exemption applies to replacement
     introduction circuits only.

WHY 16 ms. On this loopback network the adaptive timeout is learned from 3-hop circuits
that build in a few milliseconds, so it settles at 15-17 ms. A replacement introduction
circuit is FOUR hops and takes roughly 25 ms to build. It was therefore past the timeout
before it could ever finish - which is why the loss rate was as high as 14%. On the real
network the margin is wider and the loss rate would be lower, but the failure mode is
the same one and it is unbounded from the operator's point of view, which is the point
of the fix.

6d. THE 6 REBUILDS STILL NOT COMPLETED IN THE AFTER RUN
--------------------------------------------------------
They are not lost to any timeout. They arrive in two groups of three, at 14:20:19 and
at 14:52:17, and each group coincides to the millisecond with a descriptor rotation:

  Sep 21 14:20:19.633 rotate_all_descriptors: Time to rotate our descriptors ...
  Sep 21 14:20:19.633 circuit_mark_for_close_: Circuit 3757106797 (id: 250) ... hs_service.c:867  (close_intro_circuits)
  Sep 21 14:20:19.657 circuit_mark_for_close_: Circuit 3574437380 (id: 258) ... hs_service.c:3939 (orig reason: 12)
  Sep 21 14:20:19.661 circuit_mark_for_close_: Circuit 2899037000 (id: 257) ... hs_service.c:3939 (orig reason: 12)
  Sep 21 14:20:19.666 circuit_mark_for_close_: Circuit 3674738716 (id: 259) ... hs_service.c:3939 (orig reason: 12)

  reason 12 = END_CIRC_REASON_NOSUCHSERVICE, from the error path of
  service_intro_circ_has_opened(): when the circuit finished building, the introduction
  point it was replacing no longer existed, because rotate_all_descriptors() had just
  retired the descriptor that held it. tor's own time-period rotation runs every 8
  minutes on this test network.

  A rebuild for an introduction point that has ceased to exist has nothing left to
  rebuild. These are not a timing defect and nothing can be done about them: the correct
  outcome is exactly what happened, the circuit is closed immediately. The same thing
  accounts for 7 of the 411 losses in the BEFORE run (the three at 20:52:17, and four
  more later) - the 7 that the timeout did NOT claim in 6b.

  A side effect worth recording: each of those six circuits also emits
    [warn] Unknown introduction point auth key on circuit N for service ...
  from the same error path. That warning is not new (it fired 7 times in the BEFORE run,
  for exactly the same 7 circuits) but it is now reached more often, because a
  replacement circuit is no longer killed by the adaptive timeout before it can open.
  Rate observed: 6 in 73 minutes, i.e. once per descriptor rotation per descriptor.

      rotation #4  x27    rotation #8  x6
  MAX_INTRO_POINT_CIRCUIT_RETRIES is 3. Rotation depth reached 8 with no introduction
  point retired for it, which is the retry-cap defence still holding (see the commit
  message of 01213d78f and evidence 2).

  `should_remove_intro_point: ... Removing it.` in the AFTER info log:  1 occurrence
      Sep 21 14:20:23.646  Intro point $D7F2E673... (retried: 4 times). Removing it.
  That is four seconds AFTER the 14:20:19 descriptor rotation, and it is not one of the
  introduction points being rotated: the auth key QmzxYnXw... on that same relay kept
  swapping at 14:22:19, 14:24:19 and 14:26:19, straight through. It is one of the three
  intro points freshly picked for the new descriptor whose first circuit did not come
  up, which tor then replaces - ordinary upstream behaviour. The same event occurred 4
  times in the 17-hour BEFORE run.

  Introduction point continuity in the AFTER run: the longest-lived auth keys swapped 7
  times in a row at a strict 120 s, and an auth key stops appearing only when tor's own
  8-minute time-period rotation retires the descriptor that held it. The first swap of a
  freshly picked introduction point is about 2 minutes after it establishes, which is
  why 195 rather than 216 swaps fit into 36 periods x 6 introduction points.


6f. MAKE BEFORE BREAK IS UNCHANGED
------------------------------------
The service still never closes the live circuit. Evidence 5 showed the introduction
relay does it, as a consequence of the duplicate-token kill when the replacement claims
the auth key; that is why most SWAPPED lines say "the previous circuit was already
gone". In the AFTER run 184 of the 195 SWAPPED lines say that and 11 say "old circuit
N is closed" - the same split as the BEFORE run (2341 and 147 of 2488). No SWAPPED line
in either run says "NOT closed (unexpected)". The only circuit this change ever closes is a
REPLACEMENT that missed our own deadline (`ABANDONED`, 0 occurrences in this run) or one
whose introduction point disappeared (6d).


6g. WHAT WAS NOT EXERCISED
----------------------------
  ABANDONED : 0. With the adaptive timeout out of the way, every replacement on this
              network established in about 25 ms, far inside the 60-second deadline
              (half of 120, clamped to [30, 120]). The abandon path is therefore
              present and reachable in code but was not exercised by this run.
  FAILED    : 0. No replacement circuit died on its own before establishing.
  BACKOFF   : 0. It takes 3 consecutive failures on one introduction point to arm it,
              and there were no consecutive failures at all.
  The immediate-retry behaviour was likewise not observed in action, for the same
  reason: there was nothing to retry.

  So this run demonstrates change 1 (the exemption) directly and quantitatively. It
  demonstrates that changes 2 and 3 do not fire when nothing goes wrong, which is what
  they should do, but it does not demonstrate them firing. Making them fire would need
  a network that can stall a circuit build for tens of seconds, which this one cannot.
