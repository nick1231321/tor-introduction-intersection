EVIDENCE 5 - MAKE BEFORE BREAK: WHO CLOSES THE OLD CIRCUIT
==========================================================

The mitigation only works if the service never closes the live introduction circuit
before its replacement exists. The design relies on the relay doing it instead: when a
second ESTABLISH_INTRO arrives under the SAME auth key, hs_circuitmap.c kills the older
circuit holding that token. So the question is: who actually closed these circuits?

5a. EVERY INTRO CIRCUIT CLOSE WAS A REMOTE DESTROY
--------------------------------------------------
Source: control-port CIRC events with PURPOSE=HS_SERVICE_INTRO, collected live for 360 s
(20:05:39-20:11:45), raw in 04-cost-rotation-ON.txt. The control port reports both the
local reason and, when the close came from the other end, REMOTE_REASON.

  14 REASON=DESTROYED
  14 REMOTE_REASON=DESTROYED

  -> 14 closes, 14 of them carrying REMOTE_REASON=DESTROYED.
  -> 0 closes without a REMOTE_REASON, i.e. the service closed none of them itself.

Every one of those closed circuits was in HS_STATE=HSSI_ESTABLISHED, i.e. it was a live,
serving introduction circuit, and it went away because the introduction relay sent a
DESTROY cell - the duplicate-token kill firing after the replacement claimed the token.

5b. THE SAME THING SEEN IN THE SERVICE'S OWN info.log
-----------------------------------------------------
All circuit close sites during the rotation-on run, with their reason codes:

    59 src/core/or/command.c:682 (orig reason: 523
    47 src/core/or/circuituse.c:1502 (orig reason: 9
    26 src/core/or/circuituse.c:1478 (orig reason: 9
    15 src/core/or/command.c:682 (orig reason: 521
     7 src/core/or/circuitbuild.c:1112 (orig reason: 9
     6 src/feature/hs/hs_service.c:867 (orig reason: 9
     3 src/feature/hs/hs_circuitmap.c:163 (orig reason: 9

Reason codes, from src/core/or/or.h in this tree:
  END_CIRC_REASON_FLAG_REMOTE = 512  -> the close came from the other end
  521 = 512 + 9  = FLAG_REMOTE | END_CIRC_REASON_FINISHED
  523 = 512 + 11 = FLAG_REMOTE | END_CIRC_REASON_DESTROYED   (a DESTROY cell arrived)
  9   = END_CIRC_REASON_FINISHED (a local close)

src/core/or/command.c:682 is the handler for an incoming DESTROY cell, and it accounts
for all 74 remote closes. The local closes at circuituse.c:1478/1502 are
tor expiring unused general-purpose circuits, unrelated to introduction points.

NOTE ON PER-CIRCUIT TIMING: it is tempting to print 'old circuit closed N ms after the
replacement established' for each rotation. That cannot be done honestly from these logs.
The service's rotation log prints a circuit's WIRE id, while circuit_mark_for_close_
prints 'Circuit 0 (id: NNN)' - the wire id has already been cleared to 0, leaving only the
global id - and within one cycle six circuits are launched within ~2 ms of each other, so
matching the two id spaces by timestamp is ambiguous. An earlier version of this file did
exactly that and produced nonsense (one row claimed a 120-second gap). It was removed.
What IS established, without any matching, is the statement above: no intro circuit was
ever closed by the service; every one was destroyed by the relay.

5c. WHY THE SWAPPED LINE SAYS 'THE PREVIOUS CIRCUIT WAS ALREADY GONE'
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Most SWAPPED lines report that the old circuit was already gone rather than naming it:
  'previous circuit was already gone': 58
  'old circuit N is closed'          : 3

This is a cosmetic logging artifact, not a behavioural problem. On this local network the
relay's DESTROY for the old circuit arrives and unregisters it a few milliseconds before
the service finishes processing INTRO_ESTABLISHED on the new one, so by the time the swap
code looks the old circuit up it is already unregistered. The ordering that matters is
still correct - the relay only sends that DESTROY because the replacement had already
presented the same auth key. To print the old circuit id here, it would have to be stashed
in the intro point struct at START instead of being looked up again at SWAPPED.
